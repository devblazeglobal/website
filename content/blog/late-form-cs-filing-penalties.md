Every company files a corporate income tax return each year: Form C-S, Form C-S (Lite) or Form C. The deadline is 30 November. For the Year of Assessment 2026, the due date is 30 November 2026.

## What happens if the return is late?

IRAS can take one or more of these actions:

- **Estimated assessment.** IRAS estimates your income from previous years or other information, and may assume it has gone up. The estimated tax must be paid within 1 month, even if you object.
- **Composition.** IRAS can offer a composition fee in place of prosecution.
- **Notice to directors.** IRAS can require the directors to appear and explain. A director who does not comply faces a fine of up to S$10,000, up to 12 months in prison, or both, for each offence.
- **Summons.** IRAS can summon the company's directors to court.

A company that has not filed for 2 years or more can face a penalty of up to twice the tax assessed, and a fine of up to S$5,000 per offence on conviction.

## Can you object to an estimated assessment?

Yes, within 2 months. You must file the tax return, the financial statements and the tax computation with the objection. Without them, IRAS will not revise the estimate.

## What if the tax is paid late?

- A **5%** penalty applies to tax not paid by the due date.
- If the tax is still unpaid 60 days later, a further **1%** applies for each completed month, up to **12%** of the unpaid tax.

IRAS can also appoint your bank or tenants to pay the tax on your behalf.

## How to stay on time

- File Estimated Chargeable Income within 3 months after your financial year end.
- Close your books early, so the tax computation is ready well before 30 November.
- Check which tax exemptions and reliefs apply before you file.
