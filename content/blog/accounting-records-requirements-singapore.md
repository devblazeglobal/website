Directors are responsible for making sure their company keeps proper accounting records. This is a duty under the Companies Act, not a choice.

## What must a company keep?

Records that explain every transaction and the company's financial position, in enough detail to prepare true and fair financial statements. In practice this means:

- Sales and purchase invoices.
- Bank statements and receipts.
- Payroll records and CPF contributions.
- Contracts, loan agreements and asset records.
- The ledgers built from these documents.

## How long must records be kept?

At least **5 years** after the end of the financial year in which the transactions were completed. IRAS sets the same 5-year period for tax records.

## What happens if records are not kept?

- **Late filings.** Without current books, the financial statements are not ready for the AGM, the annual return or the tax return. Each late filing has its own penalty.
- **Estimated tax.** If a return is not filed, IRAS can estimate the company's income and issue an assessment, which must be paid.
- **Disallowed expenses.** An expense without supporting documents may not be accepted as a tax deduction.
- **Directors' liability.** Breaching directors' duties can lead to fines, prosecution and disqualification from acting as a director.

## Why up-to-date books help the business

Penalties are only part of it. Current books show cash flow, margins and money owed to you each month, so decisions are based on real figures, not estimates.
