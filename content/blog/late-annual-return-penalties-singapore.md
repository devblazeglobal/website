Every Singapore company must hold its AGM and file an annual return with ACRA on time each year. A missed date costs money first, then can put the company and its directors at risk.

## What is the deadline?

A private company files its annual return within 7 months after its financial year end. A company with a 31 December year end files by 31 July. You can work out your own dates with our [compliance deadline calculator](/compliance-deadline-calculator).

## How much is the late fee?

ACRA charges a late lodgement fee for annual lodgments due on or after 14 January 2022:

- **S$300** if the annual return is filed within 3 months after the deadline.
- **S$600** if it is filed more than 3 months after the deadline.

The fee applies even if the filing is only a few days late.

## What else can ACRA do?

The late fee is not the only consequence. ACRA may:

- Offer a composition sum or prosecute the directors for each breach. A late AGM and a late annual return are separate breaches.
- Debar a director who fails to lodge required documents for a continuous period of 3 months or more. A debarred person cannot take on new appointments as a director or company secretary.
- Disqualify a director convicted of 3 or more filing offences within 5 years.
- Strike the company off the register if it appears not to be carrying on business. Missing annual returns is a common trigger.

A director of 3 or more companies struck off by ACRA within 5 years is also disqualified from acting as a director of any company.

## How to stay on time

- Know your financial year end and the four dates that follow from it: ECI, AGM, annual return and corporate tax.
- Keep your books up to date through the year, so the financial statements are ready before the AGM.
- Appoint a company secretary who tracks the dates and files for you.
